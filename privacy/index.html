2. What My Word does

My Word is a goal and accountability app. You create Challenges, track Wins and Slip-ups, and optionally involve other people. A Pledge is optional, private, and not a debt. “Support My Word” is a separate optional contribution.

My Word does not provide medical, nutritional, psychological, or financial advice. It does not read Apple Health, Google Fit, contacts, or location.

3. Information My Word uses

3.1 Account and authentication

Sign-up asks for a display name, email address, and password (at least 6 characters). Authentication is handled by Supabase Auth. The app stores the display name in your account metadata and in a profile. Sign-in uses email and password. Password reset and email change are available in the account settings. Apple sign-in and Google sign-in are not implemented. The app does not ask for your age or date of birth.

3.2 Profile and photos

Profiles store a display name (unique without regard to capital letters). A deleted display name is reserved so it cannot be reused. On web, a profile photo can be stored as image data on your profile. On the current iOS and Android build, photo picking is not available. People who can see your profile in the product may see that photo.

3.3 Challenges, Wins, Slip-ups, and history

A Challenge can include the title, the words you typed, dates, repeat pattern, required Wins, Slip-up allowance, outcome, and status. Check-ins record Wins and Slip-ups and can include a note. Progress is self-reported.

3.4 Friends, invitations, and Joint Challenges

My Word does not offer a public directory of all users. Invitation links use tokens. People you invite, or who join a Joint Challenge with you, can see Challenge progress according to the product rules. Pledge amounts are not shared with followers. Share actions use the device share sheet and can include the Challenge title and a link, not the Pledge.

3.5 Pledges, settlements, and Support My Word

A Pledge is optional. No card payment is taken when a Challenge is created. If a Challenge is unsuccessful, you may be invited to honour an amount, choose a lower amount, or settle at £0. Card payments are processed by Stripe. My Word stores amounts, statuses, and Stripe identifiers (such as PaymentIntent ids). Card numbers and CVCs are entered in Stripe’s fields and are not stored as card numbers in My Word’s own tables. Currency for these flows is GBP.

Whether a stored Stripe webhook payload contains card brand, last four digits, billing details, or email: needs inspection of a redacted payload before claiming a specific shape.

Stripe’s own systems hold the payment method. Those records are not deleted by My Word’s account-deletion function.

3.6 Analytics and Secret Sauce

Product analytics can store event names, properties, platform, app version, and sometimes Challenge ids. Some analytics rows keep a record after account deletion with the user id cleared. The “Secret Sauce” setup step stores a variant and related Challenge-shape fields linked to your account; those rows are removed when the profile is deleted (cascade). There is no advertising SDK and no crash-reporting SDK such as Sentry or Firebase Crashlytics in the app package.

3.7 Device storage, notifications, and feedback

Auth sessions are persisted on the device. Notification preferences can be stored. Local reminders may be scheduled on iOS and Android after permission is granted. Feedback can include text, platform, app version, and an optional screenshot stored in private cloud storage under your user id.

3.8 Reports and blocks

Where enabled in the product, you can report content and block another person. Reports are read by MY WORD APP LTD at info@myword-app.com. The target is an initial response within 3 business days. That is a first reply, not a promise of resolution in that time.

7. Security

  • Row level security is enabled on application tables.
  • Payment creation requires a signed-in user.
  • The Stripe webhook rejects missing or invalid Stripe signatures.
  • Stripe secret keys are server secrets, not public client values.
  • Clients cannot mark a settlement as succeeded; the webhook writes that status.

This policy does not claim named certifications (for example SOC 2 or ISO 27001) that are not evidenced in the product repository.
